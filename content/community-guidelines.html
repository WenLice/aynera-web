Community Guidelines

Aynera is small, verified, and curated by people. That only works if members treat each other — and the places we meet in — with care. These Guidelines are what you agree to when you submit your profile for review, and what we refer to when we act on a report.

Safety explains what Aynera does to protect you. This page is the other half: what we ask of you. Terms of Use remains the contract; where the two overlap, the Terms govern.

Version 1.0. Last updated: 19 September 2026. Language: English.

1. Who these apply to

Every member, everywhere Aynera takes you.

These Guidelines apply to your profile, your messages, and your behaviour at any meeting or gathering that began on Aynera — including after the conversation ends. Conduct offline is not outside our scope simply because it happened offline.

2. Be who you say you are

One real person, honestly presented.

  • Use your own name, your own current photos, and your own voice and video
  • Do not impersonate anyone, or imply an employer, role, or affiliation that is not yours
  • Do not work around phone, email, or liveness verification, or ask anyone else to complete it for you
  • One account per person. Do not create another after a restriction or removal
  • Do not present yourself as available when you are not

Verification is what lets a stranger agree to meet you. Every shortcut around it spends trust that someone else built.

3. Eighteen and over

No exceptions, and no benefit of the doubt.

Aynera is for adults. If you believe a member is under 18, report it — do not handle it yourself. Reports about age go to a person, not a filter, and we would rather review a hundred mistaken reports than miss one.

4. Meet in public — and remember you are a guest

Public first meets, and care for the venues that host us.

  • For a first meet, choose a busy public place. Where we suggest a partner venue, that is a recommendation — the choice stays yours
  • Do not push someone towards a private or isolated place, or change the venue at short notice without their clear agreement
  • Treat a partner venue and its staff well. Arrive close to the agreed time, order something, and leave the table as you would want to find it

Partner cafés and event spaces agree to host Aynera members. That arrangement is ours to lose on your behalf, so how you behave in one is a matter for us and not only between you and the venue.

5. A plan is a promise

Show up, or say you cannot — in good time.

  • Agree a venue and time, then keep to it
  • If plans change, say so as early as you can. Late is better than silent
  • Repeated no-shows without notice are a conduct matter, not bad luck

Someone rearranged an evening for that meeting, and a venue held a table. Vanishing costs both of them something real.

6. Consent, pressure, and blocking

Ask, accept the answer, and leave when told to.

  • Do not pressure anyone for contact details, photographs, money, or intimacy of any kind
  • Ask before photographing or recording anyone, including at gatherings
  • Never share intimate images of another person without their clear permission. Sexual content involving minors is reported to the authorities without exception
  • Do not share anyone's private information — their address, workplace, or anything else they told you in confidence
  • A block ends contact. Do not reach that person through another account, another platform, or a mutual acquaintance

Interest that has to be repeated after a clear no is not persistence. Withdrawal is always an available answer here, including inside Focus and Together.

7. Passes are private

Not every introduction becomes a match, and that is by design.

Aynera curates introductions and only a mutual yes creates a match. When someone passes, that stays between them and us — permanently. Do not try to work out who passed on you, and do not ask our team to tell you. Refusing to say is not us being unhelpful; it is the promise that lets people be honest instead of polite.

8. Gatherings

A room of strangers deserves more care, not less.

  • Follow the host's directions and the venue's rules
  • Ask before taking photographs of anyone present
  • No one owes you a conversation, a pairing, or their contact details
  • Approaching someone repeatedly after they have stepped away is harassment

9. Report honestly

Tell us what happened — and only what happened.

  • Report a profile, a message, or an offline incident as soon as you reasonably can. Reporting stays open after a meeting has ended
  • Serious reports are reviewed by a trained person, not a filter alone
  • Reporting someone in good faith will never count against you, even if we find nothing
  • Filing a knowingly false report to damage someone is itself a breach of these Guidelines

If you are in immediate danger, contact local emergency services first. We can wait; that cannot.

10. When a guideline is broken

Proportionate, explained, and appealable.

Depending on what happened and what came before it, we may remove content, issue a warning, pause your introductions, restrict your account, or remove it permanently. Serious harm — violence, sexual content involving minors, credible threats — means immediate removal and, where appropriate, cooperation with law enforcement.

We will tell you what rule was applied. If you believe we got it wrong, reply through our grievance channel and a person will look again.

11. Changes to these Guidelines

A new version means we ask you again.

These Guidelines are versioned. When we publish a materially new version, your existing acceptance no longer stands and we will ask you to read and accept the new one before you appear in further introductions. Your acceptance of the Terms and the Privacy Policy is recorded separately and is unaffected.

Questions about anything here? Our grievance channel reaches a person.
